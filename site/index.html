See what your competitors changed, and why it matters to you.

Competitor Pulse watches your competitors' pricing pages, changelogs, homepages and careers pages. When something changes, an AI analyst decides whether it matters to your business and writes you a brief.

Setup takes one field: your website.

Set up in minutes, then hear about it when something moves

  1. 1

    Enter your website

    Competitor Pulse reads your site, drafts your company profile and suggests your direct competitors, each with a reason. You approve the shortlist.

  2. 2

    It watches the pages that matter

    For each competitor it picks the pages that carry signal and re-reads them every week. Only pages whose text actually changed go to the analyst.

  3. 3

    Review, then send

    You get a brief with each change scored and explained. Dismiss anything off-base, approve the rest, and it goes to Slack or email.

Every finding comes with the evidence

The analyst quotes the page text behind each finding, links to the source and suggests what to do about it. You can watch it read the changes as it works.

Signal, not a feed of diffs

Scored against your business
Each change is rated 1 to 5 against your company profile. A rival cutting prices for your customers ranks above a new blog post.
Nothing goes out without you
Findings wait for your review. Only what you approve reaches your team, so a wrong call never lands in the channel.
No repeats
Before it writes, the analyst checks what it has already reported about that competitor. Last month's news stays in last month.
Quiet weeks cost nothing
Pages are compared before any AI runs. When nothing changed, there is nothing to analyze and nothing to pay for.
See every step
Follow what the analyst reads and decides while it works, and see what each check cost.
More than one product
Track several companies or product lines, each with its own profile, competitors and recipients.

Delivered where your team already reads

Approved briefs go to a Slack channel, an email list or both. Each one leads with the headline and links back to the full report.

  • Slack

    Post to any channel with an incoming webhook.

  • Email

    Send to as many recipients as you like.

  • Claude and other AI assistants

    Connect over MCP and ask "what did our competitors change on pricing this month?"

Safe to point at the open web

  • Page text is data, not instructions

    Instructions hidden in a competitor's page are ignored, and we test for exactly that.

  • Narrow permissions

    Each agent gets a few tools for one job, with no shell or file access.

  • Hard limits

    Every run has a spending cap and a step limit it cannot exceed.

  • Verified links only

    A page is loaded before it is tracked, so made-up URLs are rejected.
